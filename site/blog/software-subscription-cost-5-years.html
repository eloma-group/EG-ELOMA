What Your Software Subscriptions Really Cost Over 5 Years

By EG Eloma19 Nov 20265 min read

Your software subscription costs over five years equal your monthly fees multiplied by 60, plus per-user charges for each new hire and yearly price rises. For example, six tools at $880 a month cost $52,800 over five years, or about $58,350 with 5% annual increases. Compare that total with custom business software before you decide.

This post shows how to add up your own subscriptions with an example you can copy, when custom business software starts to cost less, and what to ask before you decide.

Why a monthly fee hides the real cost

A $45 per user fee looks small. But it repeats every month, for every person, for as long as you use the tool. Three things push the total up over time:

  • Per-user pricing. Every new hire adds to the bill.
  • Add-ons. Reporting, extra storage or integrations are often priced separately.
  • Price rises. The vendor sets the price, and you can't negotiate it down by much.

There is also a cost that never appears on an invoice: staff time. When customer details live in one app and invoices in another, someone re-types the same information.

A 5-year example you can copy

These numbers are made up to show the method. They are not EG Eloma prices and not a quote. Replace them with your own.

A business with 10 staff pays for six tools:

Example monthly software subscription costs for a business with 10 staff
ToolHow it is chargedMonthly cost (AUD)
CRM$45 per user × 10 users$450
InvoicingFlat fee$60
BookingsFlat fee$80
Stock and ordersFlat fee$120
Rostering and timesheets$8 per user × 10 users$80
Reporting add-onFlat fee$90
Total per month$880

That is $10,560 a year, or $52,800 over five years if nothing changes. If prices rise 5% a year, the five-year total is about $58,350. Each new hire also adds $53 a month ($636 a year) in per-user fees for the CRM and rostering tools.

The method is three steps:

  1. List every tool and what it costs per month.
  2. Multiply by 60 months, then add a price rise you think is realistic.
  3. Add the cost of each new person you expect to hire.

When custom software starts to cost less

Custom software has a build cost up front, so it is not cheaper for every business. It tends to make sense when:

  • you pay for several overlapping tools that don't share data
  • your fees grow with every new employee
  • your process doesn't fit off-the-shelf software, so staff use workarounds
  • you want to own the system and its data instead of renting it

If you only use one or two simple tools at a low monthly cost, subscriptions are probably the better choice. A good developer should tell you that.

The comparison to make is simple: the five-year subscription total against the build cost plus hosting and support. Ask for a fixed written quote so you know the build number before you decide.

Tax treatment can differ too. The ATO says software subscriptions such as accounting, inventory and client management tools are usually operating expenses, claimed in the year you incur them. Software developed for your own business use may be claimed over time or through a software development pool. Read the ATO guidance on digital product expenses and check with your accountant before you decide.

Questions to ask before you decide

Business Victoria suggests listing your must-have and nice-to-have features, and budgeting for add-ons, training and support, not just the purchase price. Use that list with these questions:

  • Who will own the source code and the data?
  • What happens to my data if I stop using this?
  • Can the system be built in stages so I see value early?
  • What will hosting, support and changes cost after launch?

Frequently asked questions

Is custom software always cheaper than subscriptions?

No. It depends on how many tools you replace, how many users you have and how complex your process is. Work out the five-year subscription total first.

How do I calculate my software subscription costs?

List each tool, multiply its monthly cost by 60, add expected price rises and the cost of new hires. The example above shows the method.

Can I replace only some of my tools?

Yes. Many businesses start with the two or three tools that cost the most or cause the most double entry. If your main problem is apps that don't share data, API integrations may be enough.

What if my business changes later?

A system you own can be changed when your process changes, instead of waiting for a vendor to add a feature.

What are the hidden costs of software subscriptions?

Per-user fees that grow with every hire, paid add-ons, yearly price rises, and staff time spent re-typing data between apps.

Are software subscriptions tax deductible in Australia?

Generally, subscriptions used to run your business can be claimed as operating expenses, in proportion to business use. The ATO explains the rules. Ask your accountant about your situation.

How long does it take to build custom business software?

It depends on how many parts of your business the system covers. We build in stages so you see working software early, and a fixed written quote sets out scope and timeline before you commit. See our custom business software page for how it works.
